Two ideas about beauty have been fighting for centuries. One says beauty is <em>in the eye of the beholder</em> - just taste, and everyone's taste is theirs. The other says there is something actually there: harmony, proportion, a fitting-together that most people sense.

Both have backing. Tastes vary wildly across time and places. And yet some things - a face with even features, a golden sunset, a well-made chair - are beautiful to nearly everybody.

A helpful middle: beauty may not be a personal opinion and it may not be a fact like 'the cat is on the mat'. It may be something in between - <em>a real thing that requires you to learn how to see it</em>. Which is why looking at art more makes more art look good.
